# Lane: LANE 6 — COLD CHAIN & HEMOVIGILANCE

## Executive summary
A sellable blood-bank system must treat cold chain and hemovigilance as continuous, auditable processes — not static fields. Cold chain requires per-component storage rules (RBC 1–6 °C; platelets 20–24 °C with continuous agitation; FFP/cryo frozen ≤ −18 °C, ideally ≤ −25 °C; thawed FFP 1–6 °C with a short shelf life; whole blood 1–6 °C or 20–24 °C if platelets are to be recovered), continuous time-series temperature logging per storage device and per transport leg, the AABB '30-minute rule' (legacy) / modern validated-time-and-temperature rule for returning issued RBC units to inventory, and automated excursion detection that quarantines affected units pending medical-director disposition. Hemovigilance requires structured capture, ISBT/IHN-conformant classification, severity grading (0–4/death), and imputability grading (definite/probable/possible/unlikely/excluded/not-assessable) for both recipient transfusion reactions (acute & delayed: AHTR, DHTR, FNHTR, allergic/anaphylactic, TACO, TRALI, TAD, TA-GVHD, PTP, TTI/sepsis, hypotensive) and donor adverse events (VVR/syncope, hematoma, nerve/arterial injury, citrate reaction in apheresis), with regulatory reporting timelines (e.g., EU SARE/SAE; FDA fatality notification within prescribed timeframes). Disposal needs a controlled reason taxonomy plus a biohazard chain-of-custody record so wastage reporting is real. Vanilla PHP/MySQL/AJAX can model and report all of this, but CANNOT by itself perform real-time sensor ingestion, alarm-on-disconnect, or guaranteed alarm delivery — those require external IoT sensors/dataloggers, a background worker, and an out-of-band notification path, all of which break the LAMP happy path and must be engineered explicitly.

## Standards referenced
- AABB Standards for Blood Banks and Transfusion Services (35th ed.) — component storage/transport temps, agitation, return-to-inventory / '30-minute rule' and validated time-temperature alternative
- FDA 21 CFR Part 606 (cGMP for blood/components) and 21 CFR 640 (additive solutions, component-specific requirements, storage)
- FDA 21 CFR 606.171 / 600.14 — biological product deviation and fatality reporting
- FDA 21 CFR Part 11 — electronic records and electronic signatures
- EU Directive 2002/98/EC (Blood Directive) and 2004/33/EC (technical requirements)
- EU Directive 2005/61/EC — traceability and serious adverse reaction/event (SARE/SAE) notification, severity and imputability annexes
- ISBT 128 (ICCBBA) — component identification and standard terminology
- ISBT/IHN (International Haemovigilance Network) Standard Definitions for Surveillance of Non-infectious Adverse Transfusion Reactions (TACO, TRALI, FNHTR, AHTR/DHTR, allergic/anaphylactic, hypotensive, TAD, etc.) and Donor Standardized Surveillance definitions
- ISBT/AABB/IHN TACO and TRALI consensus definitions (2018/2019 revisions)
- WHO Blood Cold Chain guidance and WHO Guidelines on Good Manufacturing Practices for Blood Establishments
- ISTARE (IHN International Surveillance of Transfusion-Associated Reactions and Events) reporting framework
- HL7 v2 (ORU/OUL) and HL7 FHIR (AdverseEvent, BiologicallyDerivedProduct, Observation) for interoperability

## Recommendations
- **[must-have] Per-component storage rule master table (replace static temperature_range)** — Create component_storage_spec keyed by component_class with canonical AABB/EU values: RBC/whole_blood store 1–6 °C, transport 1–10 °C, shelf life by additive (CPDA-1 35 d, AS-1/AS-3/AS-5 42 d); platelets 20–24 °C WITH continuous gentle agitation, max 5 d (7 d with approved pathogen-reduction/testing), agitation-interruption tolerance limited (cumulative ≤24 h, single ≤30 min per common SOPs — make configurable); FFP/PF24/cryo frozen at ≤ −18 °C (preferably ≤ −25 °C for 36 mo vs 3 mo), thawed FFP 1–6 °C use within 24 h, thawed cryo 20–24 °C use within 4–6 h; granulocytes 20–24 °C transfuse ASAP (<24 h). Columns: target_min_c, target_max_c, transport_min_c, transport_max_c, requires_agitation(bool), agitation_max_interruption_min, frozen_threshold_c, shelf_life_hours, max_temp_excursion_min, source_standard. Drive ALL alarm logic and the 30-minute/return rule from this table, never hardcode.
- **[must-have] Continuous temperature time-series logging model** — Add storage_equipment (id, branch_id, type[fridge|platelet_incubator_agitator|freezer|ULT_freezer|transport_cooler|validated_shipper], component_class_allowed, target spec FK, sensor_id, calibration_due, status). Add temperature_log (id, equipment_id, sensor_id, recorded_at[UTC], temp_c, humidity, agitation_ok(bool, for platelets), source[sensor|manual], reading_uuid, ingest_received_at). Index (equipment_id, recorded_at). Expect a reading every 1–5 min per device; this is high-volume — partition by month or roll up to temperature_log_archive. Each manual reading must be operator-attributed for 21 CFR Part 11. Provide AJAX endpoints for sensor POST (token-authed) and for dashboards to poll latest + min/max per window. Also log agitator on/off and door-open events where the device exposes them.
- **[must-have] Excursion detection, quarantine, and disposition workflow** — Add temperature_excursion (id, equipment_id, component_class, started_at, ended_at, min_temp_c, max_temp_c, duration_min, threshold_breached[low|high|frozen|agitation_stopped], detected_by[auto|manual], severity, status[open|under_review|resolved], disposition[return_to_use|quarantine|discard], reviewed_by, reviewed_at, justification). On breach beyond spec tolerance, auto-create excursion AND auto-set all affected blood_units.status='QUARANTINED' (new status distinct from available/reserved/issued), linked via excursion_unit_link. Units cannot be issued while quarantined. Only blood-bank medical director / qualified reviewer role may release (return_to_use) or discard, with mandatory justification — enforced by RBAC and written to audit_logs. Affected-unit determination = all units in that equipment during the excursion window.
- **[must-have] Equipment alarm + sensor-health model with out-of-band delivery** — Add equipment_alarm (id, equipment_id, alarm_type[high_temp|low_temp|agitation_failure|power_loss|door_open|sensor_disconnect|calibration_overdue|battery_low], triggered_at, cleared_at, ack_by, ack_at, escalation_level, notes). CRITICAL GAP: a request-driven PHP app cannot detect a sensor that STOPS reporting (the dangerous failure mode) nor deliver alarms when no one is logged in. Require (a) a cron/daemon worker that flags equipment with no reading in N minutes as sensor_disconnect, (b) escalation tiers with timeouts, (c) out-of-band notification (SMS/email/voice via gateway) independent of the browser. State plainly that alarm reliability is a regulated safety function and the LAMP stack alone is insufficient without this background + notification infrastructure and ideally hardware datalogger alarms as the primary safety layer.
- **[must-have] Issued-unit return rule (30-minute / validated time-temperature)** — Model unit issue/return as events with timestamps. On return of an RBC unit, evaluate against component_storage_spec: legacy '30-minute rule' = if time out of controlled storage >30 min, do not return to inventory; modern AABB approach permits a validated time-and-temperature limit (e.g., surface temp ≤10 °C). Add unit_return_check (unit_id, issued_at, returned_at, minutes_out, measured_return_temp_c, rule_applied, decision[return_to_inventory|quarantine|discard], decided_by). Make the rule (minutes vs measured-temp) configurable per branch SOP. Platelets/thawed products generally not returnable — encode per component.
- **[must-have] Hemovigilance — recipient transfusion-reaction model** — Add transfusion_reaction (id, patient_id, unit_id(s) via reaction_unit_link, reported_by, onset_datetime, transfusion_start/stop, reaction_category, time_class[acute<24h|delayed], signs_symptoms(JSON checklist: fever, chills, dyspnea, hypotension, hypertension, urticaria, hemoglobinuria, etc.), vitals_pre/post, severity_grade[0..4|death], imputability[definite|probable|possible|unlikely|excluded|not_assessable], investigation_findings, clerical_check_result, lab_results(DAT, repeat crossmatch, cultures, NT-proBNP, etc.), outcome, reportable_to_authority(bool), report_ref, status). reaction_category enumerates ISBT/IHN types: AHTR, DHTR/DSTR, FNHTR, allergic, anaphylactic, TACO, TRALI, TAD, hypotensive, TA-GVHD, PTP, TT-sepsis/bacterial, TTI(viral/parasitic), hemosiderosis, other/unknown. Distinguish TACO vs TRALI explicitly (different timing, BNP, fluid balance, oxygenation criteria). Auto-quarantine co-component units from the same donation pending investigation. Enforce regulatory reporting flags/timelines (EU SARE; FDA fatality reporting; national hemovigilance).
- **[must-have] Hemovigilance — donor adverse-event model** — Add donor_adverse_event (id, donor_id, donation_id, event_type[VVR/syncope, prolonged_recovery, hematoma, arterial_puncture, nerve_injury/irritation, delayed_syncope_with_injury, citrate_reaction(apheresis), hemolysis(apheresis), air_embolism, allergic, generalized, local_other], onset[immediate|delayed], severity[mild|moderate|severe], with_loss_of_consciousness(bool), with_injury(bool), intervention, outcome, off_site(bool), recovery, follow_up, reported_by, reportable(bool)). Align grading to AABB/IHN-ISBT Donor Standardized Surveillance definitions. Feed donor deferral logic (e.g., severe VVR may alter future donation guidance) and branch QA dashboards. Capture denominators (donations by type) so rates per 1000/10000 donations can be reported.
- **[must-have] Severity + imputability as shared graded vocabularies** — Implement reusable lookup tables hv_severity_grade (0 no sign/none, 1 minor, 2 moderate/hospitalization, 3 severe/life-threatening, 4 death) and hv_imputability (definite/certain, probable, possible, unlikely, excluded, not assessable) referenced by both recipient and donor events, matching EU Directive 2005/61/EC Annex and ISBT/IHN guidance. Reports (rate, severity distribution, imputability distribution per category) depend on these being controlled vocabularies, not free text.
- **[must-have] Discard/disposal reason taxonomy + biohazard chain-of-custody** — Add discard_reason lookup with coded taxonomy: EXPIRED, EXCURSION_OUT_OF_RANGE, FAILED_VISUAL_INSPECTION(clots/hemolysis/lipemia/discoloration/leak), POSITIVE_INFECTIOUS_MARKER(HIV/HBV/HCV/syphilis/etc.), QUARANTINE_NOT_RELEASED, BROKEN_COLD_CHAIN_IN_TRANSPORT, RETURN_RULE_FAILED(>30min), CONTAMINATION/POSITIVE_CULTURE, LABEL/CLERICAL_ERROR, LEAKAGE/INTEGRITY_BREACH, QNS/LOW_VOLUME, DONOR_POST_DONATION_INFO, RECALL/LOOKBACK, EQUIPMENT_FAILURE, OTHER(free text required). Add unit_disposal (id, unit_id, disposal_reason_code, decided_by, authorized_by, disposal_datetime, method[autoclave|incineration|licensed_biohazard_contractor], biohazard_batch_id, manifest_ref, witnessed_by, weight_kg). Add biohazard_disposal_batch (id, branch_id, created_at, contractor, manifest_number, sealed_by, collected_at, certificate_ref). This makes the wastage report quantitative (discards by reason, by component, by branch, expiry rate, excursion-loss rate) and satisfies regulated waste tracking.
- **[should-have] Transport/shipment cold-chain capture** — For inter-branch transfer and hospital dispatch, add shipment_cold_chain (shipment_id, container_id, validated_shipper(bool), packout_config, departure_temp_c, departure_time, arrival_temp_c, arrival_time, embedded_logger_id, logger_data_ref, excursion_detected(bool)). Validated shippers/temperature-monitored transport are expected by buyers; on arrival, an excursion forces receiving-branch quarantine before the units re-enter available inventory.
- **[should-have] 21 CFR Part 11 controls over all hemovigilance/cold-chain records** — Every disposition (release from quarantine, discard authorization, reaction classification, return-to-inventory) must be an attributed, timestamped, reason-bearing audit_logs entry; records should be append-only with electronic signatures (signed_by, signature_meaning, signed_at) and tamper-evidence (row hashing). MySQL alone does not give you immutability — add application-enforced no-update/no-delete on these tables plus periodic hash-chaining, and say plainly that true WORM/Part-11 compliance needs configuration discipline (DB privileges, signed audit trail) beyond default LAMP.
- **[nice-to-have] HL7/FHIR alignment for interoperability** — Map reactions to FHIR AdverseEvent + (where applicable) the blood/transfusion context, units to BiologicallyDerivedProduct, and patient/order to ServiceRequest/Encounter; expose temperature observations as FHIR Observation. Even if MVP uses internal tables, design column semantics to map cleanly so a hospital's LIS/EHR integration (a common procurement requirement) is feasible later via HL7 v2 OUL/ORU or FHIR APIs.

## Data model changes
- component_storage_spec — per-component canonical temp/agitation/shelf-life rules; FK target for all alarm and return logic (replaces static temperature_range)
- storage_equipment — each fridge/freezer/platelet agitator-incubator/transport cooler with sensor_id, calibration_due, allowed component_class, status
- temperature_log — high-volume time-series (equipment_id, recorded_at UTC, temp_c, humidity, agitation_ok, source, reading_uuid); partition/archive by month
- temperature_excursion — start/end, min/max, duration, threshold_breached, severity, status, disposition, reviewer, justification
- excursion_unit_link — many-to-many excursion↔affected blood_units
- equipment_alarm — alarm_type incl. sensor_disconnect/power_loss/agitation_failure, ack/escalation fields
- sensor / sensor_health — last_seen_at heartbeat for disconnect detection by background worker
- unit_return_check — issued/returned timestamps, minutes_out, return temp, 30-min/validated-temp rule decision
- blood_units: add status value 'QUARANTINED' and 'DISCARDED'; add current_equipment_id, quarantine_reason FK
- transfusion_reaction + reaction_unit_link — recipient events: category, time_class, severity_grade, imputability, investigation, lab results, reportable flags
- donor_adverse_event — donor events: type, onset, severity, LOC/injury, intervention, outcome, reportable, follow-up
- hv_severity_grade, hv_imputability — shared graded lookup vocabularies (EU 2005/61/EC, ISBT/IHN)
- discard_reason — coded disposal reason taxonomy lookup
- unit_disposal — per-unit disposal record (reason, authorizer, method, witnessed_by, weight) linked to biohazard batch
- biohazard_disposal_batch — contractor manifest, certificate_ref, chain-of-custody for waste
- shipment_cold_chain — transport leg temps, embedded logger data, excursion flag (extends inter-branch transfer/dispatch)
- electronic_signature columns/table — signed_by, signature_meaning, signed_at + row hash for 21 CFR Part 11 on dispositions

## PHP/MySQL feasibility
Modeling, classification, disposition workflows, disposal taxonomy, and all hemovigilance/wastage REPORTING are fully feasible in vanilla PHP/MySQL/AJAX and this is where the stack is adequate. Where it BREAKS for the sellable bar: (1) Continuous sensor ingestion and especially detection of sensor/power LOSS — a request-driven app sees nothing when nothing is sent; you must add a background worker (cron/daemon/queue consumer) outside PHP's request lifecycle. (2) Guaranteed alarm delivery — alarms must reach staff when no one is logged in, requiring an out-of-band SMS/email/voice gateway and escalation engine; browser-based alerts are insufficient and arguably unsafe as the sole mechanism. (3) Real-time monitoring — vanilla PHP has no native push; AJAX polling is a workaround with lag and load cost. (4) High-volume time-series storage — needs partitioning/archival strategy MySQL can do but not by default. (5) 21 CFR Part 11 immutability and electronic signatures — require enforced append-only DB privileges, hash-chaining, and signature semantics layered on top; not provided by default LAMP. (6) Calibrated, validated hardware dataloggers with independent alarms should remain the PRIMARY safety layer regardless of software — the software is the record/QA/reporting system, not the safety instrument. Plainly: build the data model and workflows in PHP/MySQL, but you MUST add a background worker, an out-of-band notification path, and lean on validated sensor hardware; selling this as a compliant cold-chain monitor without those is not honest.

## Risks & tradeoffs
- Request-driven PHP cannot detect a sensor that stops reporting or deliver alarms when no browser session is open — the most dangerous cold-chain failures (power loss, sensor disconnect) require a background daemon/cron + out-of-band SMS/voice notification; the LAMP stack alone is NOT a compliant alarm system. Hardware dataloggers with independent alarms should remain the primary safety layer.
- Temperature logging is high write-volume (per-device readings every 1–5 min × many devices × multi-branch) — MySQL will need partitioning, rollups, and retention policy; naive single-table inserts will degrade. Time-series workloads are not LAMP's strength.
- Real-time dashboards via AJAX polling add load and still lag true real-time; WebSockets/SSE are not native to vanilla PHP and need extra infrastructure.
- 21 CFR Part 11 immutability/electronic-signature requirements are not met by default MySQL — needs enforced append-only privileges, hash-chaining, and signed audit trail; buyers in regulated markets will audit this.
- Standards values (platelet 7-day, return-rule specifics, agitation interruption limits) vary by edition/jurisdiction and approved devices — must be configurable per branch SOP, not hardcoded, or the product will be non-compliant somewhere.
- TACO vs TRALI differentiation requires clinical data (BNP, fluid balance, oxygenation, imaging) the system may not natively capture — needs structured clinical fields and clinician input, raising data-entry burden.
- Offline-sync queue + cold-chain alarms conflict: a branch offline cannot raise/deliver real-time excursion alarms; safety-critical alarming must not depend on central sync.
- Sensor vendor heterogeneity (Modbus/MQTT/REST/CSV dataloggers) means the ingest endpoint needs adapters; assuming one protocol will break field deployments.
