# Lane: LANE 7 — DEMAND FORECASTING & ANALYTICS

## Executive summary
A sellable blood-bank analytics lane must do two distinct things that the plan currently conflates. (1) FORECASTING: predict short-horizon (7–28 day) demand per blood-group×component to drive reorder/collection targets and reservation buffers. For blood banks the practical, defensible methods are moving average, simple exponential smoothing (SES), and Holt-Winters (triple exponential smoothing) for weekly seasonality; heavier ML (ARIMA, gradient boosting, LSTM) is rarely justified at a single hospital's data volume and adds validation, explainability, and 21 CFR Part 11 burden without clear ROI. (2) ANALYTICS/KPIs: compute operational quality indicators a hospital buyer evaluates during procurement — stock-out rate, wastage/expiry rate, crossmatch-to-transfusion (C:T) ratio, days-of-supply, turnaround time, donor return rate, near-expiry inventory. The hard requirement underneath both is a clean, timestamped event log: every state transition of a unit (collected, tested, available, reserved, crossmatched, issued, transfused, returned, discarded, expired, transferred) and every request must be recorded with timestamps, actor, and reason codes. Without that event spine, KPIs are unreliable and forecasting has nothing to learn from. Vanilla PHP/MySQL is fully adequate for KPI computation and for the lightweight forecasting models if run as scheduled jobs (cron) rather than per-request; the gap is that PHP has no native time-series/statistics library, so Holt-Winters and confidence intervals must be hand-coded and unit-tested, and a true scheduler (not request-triggered cron emulation) is required for reproducible nightly aggregation.

## Standards referenced
- AABB Standards for Blood Banks and Transfusion Services (quality indicators, C:T ratio, wastage/expiry monitoring)
- WHO Blood Safety / Action Framework (wastage targets, inventory management, days-of-supply guidance)
- ISBT 128 (unit identification underpinning per-unit event tracking and traceability)
- HL7 / FHIR (BiologicallyDerivedProduct, SupplyDelivery, ServiceRequest resources for interoperable event/issue records)
- FDA 21 CFR Part 11 (append-only audit trail, reproducibility of computed/forecast values, electronic records integrity)
- EU Blood Directive 2002/98/EC and 2005/61/EC (traceability and haemovigilance event recording requirements)
- MSBOS / Maximum Surgical Blood Order Schedule and Transfusion Index (C:T ratio context and crossmatch ordering benchmarks)

## Recommendations
- **[must-have] Forecast at the granularity that drives action: group × component × site, daily, short horizon** — Forecast demand (issues/transfusions) separately per ABO/Rh group (8 groups) × component (RBC, platelets/PLT, FFP, cryo) × branch, on a daily bucket with a rolling 7–28 day horizon. Do NOT forecast total blood demand — it is useless for ordering because O-neg and platelets are the binding constraints. Platelets need their own short horizon (5–7 day shelf life) vs RBC (35–42 day). Output per series: point forecast, and an upper safety quantile (e.g., 90th–95th percentile of demand) used to set the reorder/collection target, because the cost of stock-out (cancelled surgery, emergency O-neg release) is asymmetrically higher than the cost of mild over-collection.
- **[must-have] Method ladder by data availability — degrade gracefully, never error on sparse data** — Implement three tiers and auto-select by history length per series: (a) <14 days history → moving average of available days plus a fixed safety multiplier (cold start, see below); (b) 14–~56 days → simple exponential smoothing (SES): L_t = α·y_t + (1−α)·L_{t−1}, forecast = L_t; α≈0.2–0.3 default, or fit by minimizing in-sample SSE via grid search 0.05–0.6. SES handles level with no trend/seasonality and is robust on short noisy series; (c) ≥2 full weekly cycles (~14+ days, ideally 8+ weeks) → Holt-Winters additive with weekly seasonality (period m=7): Level L_t=α(y_t−S_{t−m})+(1−α)(L_{t−1}+T_{t−1}); Trend T_t=β(L_t−L_{t−1})+(1−β)T_{t−1}; Season S_t=γ(y_t−L_t)+(1−γ)S_{t−m}; Forecast ŷ_{t+h}=L_t+h·T_t+S_{t−m+((h−1) mod m)+1}. Use ADDITIVE (not multiplicative) seasonality — blood demand has additive weekday effects and additive avoids divide-by-zero on low-volume rare groups. Initialize L_0 = mean of first week, S = first-week deviations, T_0 = (mean week2 − mean week1)/7.
- **[must-have] Cold-start: ship clinically-grounded defaults so day-one is usable without history** — On install, seed forecasts from population priors, not zeros. Defaults: (1) ABO/Rh distribution constants to apportion total demand into groups when only aggregate volume is known — region-agnostic default Caucasian-ish blend O+ 37%, A+ 36%, B+ 8%, AB+ 3%, O− 7%, A− 6%, B− 1.5%, AB− 0.5% — but EXPOSE these as an editable config table because distributions are highly region/ethnicity-dependent (e.g., much higher B in South Asia, near-zero Rh-neg in East Asia); shipping one country's numbers hard-coded is a defect. (2) Days-of-supply targets as configurable defaults: RBC 5–7 days, platelets 2–3 days (shelf-life constrained), FFP/cryo larger. (3) During cold start, demand proxy = each branch's own historical issue volume if migrating from a prior system (import CSV), else manual admin-entered expected daily usage per group×component. (4) Blend prior with observed data using a weight that decays as real history accrues (e.g., weight_on_prior = max(0, (N_min − n_days)/N_min)). Never present a forecast as confident until ≥14 days of real data; flag it as 'provisional'.
- **[must-have] Compute the procurement-relevant KPIs with explicit, defensible formulas** — Wastage/Expiry rate = units discarded (any reason) / units that left available stock, AND a sub-metric expiry-specific wastage = units expired-on-shelf / units available; WHO/AABB benchmark target typically <5% for RBC, with platelets higher due to shelf life. Stock-out rate = (# of group×component×day cells where on-hand=0 OR an urgent request could not be met) / total cells; track separately the more serious 'unmet urgent request' count. Crossmatch-to-Transfusion (C:T) ratio = units crossmatched / units actually transfused; target ≤2.0–2.5 (lower is better; high C:T signals over-ordering and reservation locking that inflates apparent demand and wastage). Transfusion probability/index and MSBOS feed off this. Days-of-supply = current on-hand units / average daily issues (trailing window). Turnaround time (TAT) = time from request received → unit issued (and sub-TATs: request→crossmatch-complete, crossmatch→issue); also collection→available TAT. Donor return rate = repeat donors in period / total donors (and lapsed-donor count for recruitment). Near-expiry inventory = units with ≤X days to expiry (X configurable per component, e.g., RBC 7d, PLT 2d) — drives FEFO issuing and inter-branch transfer triggers. Report each KPI per branch and rolled-up, with trend sparkline and target/threshold coloring.
- **[must-have] Run forecasting and heavy aggregation as scheduled jobs, not per-request** — Nightly cron job: (1) materialize a daily fact table (see data model) by aggregating the event log; (2) recompute each series' forecast and write to a forecast table with model_used, params (α,β,γ), as_of_date, horizon, point, and safety_quantile; (3) recompute KPI snapshots into a kpi_daily table. Dashboards then read pre-aggregated tables → sub-second loads and reproducible numbers. Do NOT compute Holt-Winters in PHP on page load. Per-request AJAX should only read cached aggregates and do trivial filtering. This also gives you a clean as-of audit trail (forecast made on date D used params P) which matters for 21 CFR Part 11 reproducibility if a clinical decision is ever questioned.
- **[should-have] Be honest about where ML does and does not belong** — ARIMA/SARIMA, Prophet, gradient boosting, and LSTM are NOT justified for a per-hospital deployment: data volume (often <100 units/day total, far fewer per rare group) is too small for stable training; they need a Python/R runtime (not in the PHP stack); they require model validation/versioning that triggers documentation and Part-11 change-control overhead; and they are hard to explain to a clinical auditor. Holt-Winters with a safety quantile captures ~all realistic signal (weekly seasonality + slow trend) at this scale. Reserve ML only for a future multi-tenant central service that pools data across many hospitals and adds exogenous regressors (surgery schedules, local trauma/holiday calendars) — and even then deliver it as a separate microservice, not inside the PHP monolith. Say plainly: shipping ML now would be cost without payback and adds regulatory surface area.
- **[should-have] Track forecast accuracy and surface it (MAPE/MASE), or the forecast is unaccountable** — Store actuals next to forecasts and compute rolling accuracy per series: MAE, and MASE (mean absolute scaled error, scaled by naive seasonal forecast — robust to low/zero-volume series where MAPE divides by zero). Show buyers a 'forecast skill' panel proving the model beats naive. Auto-fallback: if Holt-Winters MASE >1 (worse than naive) for a series over the last N days, revert that series to SES or moving average. This guards against Holt-Winters overfitting noise on thin rare-group data.
- **[should-have] Drive operational actions from analytics, don't just display them** — Close the loop: near-expiry + low-demand-forecast at branch A combined with stock-out-risk forecast at branch B should auto-suggest an inter-branch transfer (ties into existing transfer module). Forecast safety_quantile vs on-hand should set the reorder/collection target and feed the existing alerts module (e.g., 'O-neg projected below 2 days supply in 3 days'). C:T ratio above threshold per ordering physician/ward should raise a stewardship flag. This converts the dashboard from a report into a selling feature.
- **[should-have] Make all targets, benchmarks, and distributions configurable per tenant** — Because the product is region-agnostic: ABO/Rh population distribution, days-of-supply targets, near-expiry windows per component, KPI thresholds (wastage %, C:T target, stock-out tolerance), seasonality period, and forecast safety quantile must all live in editable config tables, defaulted from cited best-practice but never hard-coded. Buyers in different regions will reject numbers that don't match their epidemiology.

## Data model changes
- unit_event_log (event spine — likely the single biggest addition): unit_id FK, event_type ENUM(collected, tested_pass, tested_fail, labeled_available, reserved, crossmatched, issued, transfused, returned_to_stock, discarded, expired, transferred_out, transferred_in, quarantined), event_ts (UTC, precise), actor_user_id, branch_id, related_request_id NULL, reason_code_id NULL, free_text NULL. Append-only, immutable (no UPDATE/DELETE) for 21 CFR Part 11. ALL KPIs and forecasts derive from this — current blood_units status fields are insufficient because they store only current state, not transition timestamps.
- reason_codes lookup: code, category (discard_reason, wastage_reason, return_reason, transfer_reason), description. Required to compute WHY units are wasted (expired vs hemolyzed vs broken cold-chain vs over-ordered) — buyers want wastage broken down by cause.
- blood_requests must capture timestamps for the TAT chain: requested_ts, crossmatch_started_ts, crossmatch_completed_ts, issued_ts, urgency ENUM(routine, urgent, emergency/MTP), requesting_ward_id, requesting_physician_id, plus outcome (fulfilled, partial, unmet, cancelled) and units_requested vs units_crossmatched vs units_transfused — needed for C:T ratio, stock-out (unmet urgent), and TAT.
- crossmatch_records (or fields on request): units_crossmatched count and link to transfusion outcome, so C:T = SUM(crossmatched)/SUM(transfused) is computable; without explicit crossmatched-but-not-transfused tracking, C:T cannot be measured.
- demand_fact_daily (materialized nightly): date, branch_id, blood_group, component, units_issued, units_transfused, units_crossmatched, units_requested, units_unmet, units_expired, units_discarded, on_hand_eod. Pre-aggregated source for forecasting and dashboards.
- forecast (output table): series_key (branch+group+component), as_of_date, horizon_day, forecast_date, point_qty, safety_quantile_qty, model_used ENUM(moving_avg, ses, holt_winters), params_json (α,β,γ, window), provisional_flag. Append/version by as_of_date for reproducibility.
- forecast_accuracy: series_key, eval_date, actual_qty, forecasted_qty, abs_error, mase — for the skill panel and auto-fallback.
- kpi_daily (snapshot): date, branch_id, group, component, plus computed KPI columns (wastage_rate, expiry_rate, stockout_rate, ct_ratio, days_of_supply, mean_tat_minutes, near_expiry_count). Pre-computed so dashboards never run heavy queries live.
- config_targets per tenant/branch: abo_rh_distribution, days_of_supply_target by component, near_expiry_window_days by component, wastage_threshold, ct_target, stockout_tolerance, safety_quantile, seasonality_period. Editable.
- donor table needs first_donation_date and a way to count donations-in-period to compute donor return/retention rate; donations already exist but ensure each links donor_id + donation_ts so repeat vs new is derivable.

## PHP/MySQL feasibility
Adequate with two clear caveats. KPI COMPUTATION: fully feasible in MySQL — wastage rate, stock-out rate, C:T ratio, days-of-supply, TAT, donor return rate, and near-expiry counts are all GROUP BY / window-function aggregations over the unit_event_log and blood_requests tables. Use MySQL 8.0+ window functions (LAG, SUM OVER, COUNT OVER) for rolling windows and EOD on-hand; pre-materialize into kpi_daily via a nightly job so dashboards read cheap. FORECASTING: moving average and SES are trivial in SQL/PHP. Holt-Winters is feasible in PHP but must be hand-coded (no library), run as a batch job over demand_fact_daily, and unit-tested against known fixtures — this is the main engineering cost. WHAT BREAKS / WHAT IS NEEDED: (1) A REAL scheduler (OS cron / Task Scheduler / daemon) is required for deterministic nightly aggregation and forecasting; request-triggered pseudo-cron is insufficient for a sellable, auditable system — state this plainly to the buyer. (2) PHP's lack of a stats library means quantiles, parameter fitting, and accuracy metrics (MASE) are bespoke code needing test coverage. (3) Anything beyond Holt-Winters (ARIMA, ML) is NOT feasible in-stack and should not be attempted in the PHP monolith; defer to a future separate Python service only if multi-tenant data pooling ever justifies it. (4) The append-only immutable event log needed for both analytics and 21 CFR Part 11 must be enforced at the DB/app layer (no UPDATE/DELETE; corrections via compensating events) — MySQL supports this via triggers/permissions but it must be deliberately designed, not assumed.

## Risks & tradeoffs
- PHP has no native, tested time-series/statistics library: Holt-Winters, parameter fitting (grid search), quantile estimation, MASE must be hand-implemented and unit-tested. Risk of subtle bugs (e.g., seasonal index drift, divide-by-zero on zero-volume rare groups) that silently corrupt forecasts. Mitigate with a fixed test fixture of known input→output series.
- True scheduling is a stack gap: reliable nightly jobs need OS cron / Windows Task Scheduler or a daemon. The 'offline-sync queue / request-triggered cron emulation' pattern common in pure-PHP shops produces non-deterministic run times and missed nights, breaking reproducibility — unacceptable for a clinical KPI/forecast that may be cited. State plainly: a real scheduler is required, not pseudo-cron.
- Thin-data instability: rare groups (AB−, O−) and platelets at small branches may have many zero-demand days. Holt-Winters and MAPE behave badly here; additive HW + MASE + auto-fallback to SES/moving-average is mandatory, not optional.
- Garbage-in: every KPI and forecast is only as good as the event log. If staff don't record transfused/returned/discarded events with timestamps and reason codes, C:T, wastage-by-cause, and TAT are all wrong. This is a workflow/UX and training risk, not just schema — capture must be low-friction (e.g., barcode scan at each transition) or data quality collapses.
- Forecasts driving auto-orders create liability: if the system recommends collection/reorder targets and a stock-out causes a clinical event, the buyer will scrutinize the model. Keep humans in the loop (suggest, not auto-execute), log as-of params, and label provisional forecasts — this is both a safety and a Part-11 reproducibility requirement.
- Region-agnostic distributions: hard-coding any single population's ABO/Rh split or benchmark target will produce wrong group-level forecasts and KPI red/green flags in other regions; must be config-driven, which adds onboarding complexity.
- C:T ratio is only meaningful if crossmatched-but-not-transfused units are explicitly tracked and returned to stock; partial implementations that only log transfusions will report a misleadingly perfect C:T and miss the wastage signal the metric exists to catch.
